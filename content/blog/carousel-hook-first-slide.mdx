---
title: The first slide is a promise, not a title
description: Why the cover of a carousel should name the change a reader gets, and how to write one that survives editing.
---

## A title describes, a promise commits

Most covers name the topic and stop there. "Notes on pricing" tells a reader what the deck is about, but gives them no reason to swipe. A promise names what will be different after page eight: "Three pricing mistakes that cost us six months."

The test is simple. Read your cover and ask what the reader can do tomorrow that they could not do today. If there is no answer, you wrote a title.

## Write the last slide first

The promise on the cover has to be one the deck actually keeps. The reliable way to guarantee that is to write the final slide before the cover, then describe that ending on page one.

- Write the closing action or conclusion.
- Compress it into one sentence a stranger could repeat.
- Put that sentence, in the reader's terms, on the cover.

## Keep it short enough to stay large

Cover text competes with every other cover in the feed, and it competes at thumbnail size. A promise that needs three lines at small type has already lost to one that fits in two lines at large type.

If the sentence will not shrink, the promise is probably two promises. Split the deck.

## Edit the cover last

Generation gives you a reasonable cover immediately, which makes it easy to accept and move on. Come back to it after the middle pages are final. The deck you end up with is rarely the deck you planned, and the promise should match what you actually wrote.
