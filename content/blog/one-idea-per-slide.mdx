---
title: One idea per slide, and what to do with the leftovers
description: A practical rule for cutting a dense draft into pages that each carry a single job.
---

## The rule

Every page gets one job. A claim, a piece of evidence, a step, a contrast — one of those, not two. A page carrying two ideas forces the reader to decide which one matters, and most readers resolve that by swiping.

## How to find pages that break the rule

Read each page aloud and count the sentences that could stand alone as a headline. If the count is above one, the page is doing too much.

Three common offenders:

- A claim and its counterargument stacked together.
- A step that quietly contains a second step.
- A number with an explanation of how the number was produced.

## What to do with the leftover

Splitting is the obvious fix, but it is not always the right one. Before you add a page, ask which of these the leftover is:

1. A second idea that deserves its own page — split it.
2. Support for the first idea that the reader does not need — cut it.
3. Support the reader does need, but not here — move it next to the claim it supports.

Most leftovers are the second kind. Decks get better by getting shorter far more often than by getting longer.

## Why this matters more after generation

A model writing from a topic will happily fill every page to the edge, because filling space looks like effort. Density is the default failure mode of a generated draft, not sparseness. Assume your first draft is too full and edit downward.
