---
title: Choosing a canvas size before you write
description: Portrait, square and widescreen each change how much text a page can hold. Pick the shape first.
---

## The shape is a budget

A canvas is not a wrapper you choose at export time. It decides how many words fit at a readable size, and that decision changes what you can say.

- 4:5 portrait is the densest feed shape. It holds a heading and a short paragraph comfortably.
- 1:1 square gives up vertical room. Expect to lose a line or two per page.
- 9:16 is tall and narrow. Long headings wrap badly here; short lines win.
- 16:9 widescreen is presentation shape. It is wide, but it is short, so a page that fit in portrait will shrink noticeably.

## Widescreen is not portrait turned sideways

The most common surprise is 16:9. Because the layouts were designed for tall pages, a widescreen page has to scale its type down to keep the same content from overflowing. The text stays legible on a laptop and gets hard to read on a phone.

Use widescreen when the deck will be shown, not scrolled.

## Pick first, then write

Changing the canvas after the words are final means re-editing the words, because the budget changed. Choosing a template sets both the canvas and the palette in one step, which is the cheapest moment to make this decision.

## When you need two shapes

If the same argument has to run in a feed and in a meeting, treat them as two decks with a shared outline rather than one deck exported twice. The outline is the reusable part; the page breaks are not.
